Best booking software for cleaning services: recurring jobs, keys and proof photos

A cleaning company does not sell appointments. It sells a standing Tuesday slot, a set of keys, and a photo of a kitchen that was actually cleaned. Most booking tools handle the calendar and nothing else.

What a cleaning business actually schedules

Four job types sit in the same week and behave differently:

  • Recurring domestic cleans - weekly or fortnightly, same client, same cleaner, same slot for years. This is the revenue base and it must never be re-entered by hand.
  • One-off deep cleans priced by rooms and condition, often booked inside 48 hours.
  • Commercial contracts that run outside opening hours and are invoiced monthly against a signed schedule.
  • Add-ons - oven, fridge, windows, laundry. Sold at booking or discovered on site, which changes price and time on the day.

Software that cannot express "every second Tuesday, skipping the holiday week, three hours, card charged the morning after" will push you back to a spreadsheet within a month. That is the test that eliminates most of the market.

The five things that matter

  1. True recurring series, not copied bookings. The client should skip one visit or pause for a holiday and the series should survive. If editing one visit breaks the pattern, you rebuild schedules every month.
  2. Access and key records. Alarm codes, key safe numbers, parking permits - a structured client field visible to the assigned cleaner only, not a free-text note every staff member can read.
  3. Photo checklists per room. Before and after photos, timestamped, on the client's own job page. This ends the "you did not clean the bathroom" conversation, and it is what general-purpose tools still get wrong.
  4. Card on file with automatic charging. Recurring cleaning only works if the card is charged after the visit without a conversation - saved payment methods plus a written policy for failed cards and late cancellations.
  5. Cleaner hours and payroll. Who has the key, how long each cleaner logged, what they are owed. Reconstructed from memory on Friday, you are losing money quietly.

Where the main tools land

Jobber is the safest default once you run two or more vans: recurring jobs, client hub, quote-to-invoice, card on file, and a mobile app cleaners use. Photos are job-level rather than a room checklist, so proof-heavy teams pair it with a checklist app.

Housecall Pro is the closest competitor and friendlier for a small residential team - faster setup, strong card processing, workable recurring scheduling.

ZenMaid is built only for cleaning companies: recurring series, cleaner assignment, client portal, review requests, and an office view designed for how a cleaning schedule really works.

BookingKoala wins if most bookings come from your own site: a real funnel with zip-code pricing, add-ons, staff matching and recurring plans. Budget a weekend for setup.

ServiceM8 and Workiz suit commercial and property-management work, where jobs arrive by phone. A solo cleaner under 20 regular clients can run Square Appointments free at that volume; it will not run a team.

Keys are a liability problem, not a scheduling problem

Every cleaner holding a key is a risk you carry. Software helps: record which key or code exists and who holds it, log issue and return dates, and restrict access notes to the assigned cleaner. It cannot make a lost key safe, so use a key safe or smart lock code per client wherever the client agrees, and change the code when a cleaner leaves. Write the note as a procedure ("key safe left of the door, code in the app, alarm off within 30 seconds"), never as a secret pasted into a client note.

Photos end more disputes than any policy

The cheapest insurance here is six to ten timestamped frames uploaded before the cleaner leaves: kitchen surfaces, hob, sink, bathroom, floors, plus anything the client flagged. They prove the visit happened and the standard met. They also sell add-ons - a photo of a filthy oven with a polite "we can add this next time for X" converts better than a price list. Price those consistently; the logic in pricing mobile services applies.

No-shows on a recurring series

A client who cancels the morning of the clean still costs you the slot, because the cleaner was already routed. Publish a 24 or 48 hour window, charge a percentage inside it, and let the software enforce it. Templates are in cutting no-shows with automated reminders.

What to pay

Expect roughly 30 to 130 USD per month per office, plus card processing near 2.6 to 2.9 percent. Per-cleaner pricing gets expensive fast.

The short version

  • Two or more vans, mixed work: Jobber or Housecall Pro.
  • Residential recurring, cleaning only: ZenMaid.
  • Website-driven bookings with zip-code pricing: BookingKoala.
  • Solo operator: Square Appointments until it hurts, then move.

Frequently asked

What is the best booking software for a small cleaning company?

ZenMaid for residential recurring work, Jobber or Housecall Pro once you run two or more vans, and Square Appointments for a solo cleaner under 20 clients.

How do I handle client keys in booking software?

Record the key or code as a structured client field restricted to the assigned cleaner, log issue and return dates, and prefer a key safe or smart lock.

Do I need before and after photos?

Yes, if you clean inside client homes. Six to ten timestamped photos per job prevent most quality disputes and make add-on sales easier.
