Best tablet POS for salons: hardware that survives a salon

The tablet is the cheapest part of the setup and the first thing to fail. What you are really buying is a stand that does not wobble, a reader that works on salon wifi, and a replacement path that does not cost a week of takings.

The tablet is not the decision

Almost every tablet made in the last five years runs a point-of-sale app. What differs at a salon counter is physical: how the device is held, how it charges, what happens when a bottle of colour hits it, and whether the card reader can walk away. A mid-range tablet in a bolted-down stand beats a flagship lying loose on a shelf. Price the whole counter — tablet, stand, reader, printer, cabling — before comparing anything else.

For a single chair, an all-in-one terminal is enough. Once you run a book with deposits, retail and two staff, a tablet pays for itself because one screen shows the day, the client notes and the payment. The trade-off: it is a general-purpose computer on a counter where people hold scissors.

What actually kills salon hardware

  • Hair in the charging port. The most common failure and the least dramatic. A stand that charges through pogo pins or induction removes the port from the equation.
  • Spray and acetone. Both end up on the screen and in the seams. A wipeable case with a raised lip matters more than a screen protector.
  • Drops from counter height. Clients knock things over while paying. A weighted base or clamp mount survives what a plastic kickstand does not.
  • Heat and sunlight. A window-facing tablet will throttle or shut down in summer. Check where the sun lands before mounting anything.

The four pieces at the counter

  1. The reader. Contactless and chip, plus a way to take a card not present for deposits and no-show fees. Test the flow you will use weekly, not just a sale.
  2. The mount. Bolted, clamped or weighted. If it can be carried to the back room, it will be.
  3. Receipts. Printed or texted. A thermal printer is one more thing to jam and feed; most salons can go digital and keep paper for exceptions.
  4. The drawer, or no drawer. If cash is a small part of takings, a lockable insert beats a full drawer cabled to the tablet.

Buy the mount and reader inside the software ecosystem where you can. Every extra brand is another support line and another firmware update.

Connectivity is part of the hardware decision

Check that the reader can use its own mobile connection or store transactions offline and settle later. Then test it: switch the wifi off mid-sale and watch what happens. Keep the POS on its own network or a guest-isolated SSID — client phones streaming video on the same router cause failed payments that look like hardware faults.

A sensible order of buying

One chair, mostly cards. Start with an all-in-one terminal and add a tablet only when you need the calendar on the counter.

Two to four chairs. One tablet in a fixed stand, one tethered reader, digital receipts, cash drawer only if you take cash. This is the setup that lasts.

Growing shop with retail. Two devices, desk and floor, but confirm stock syncs between them before buying the second.

Running costs nobody quotes

The hardware receipt is the small number. The annual one includes paper rolls, a replacement case after the first drop, per-device subscriptions, reader fees on top of card fees, and a spare so a failure does not close the desk. Budget that spare reader from day one: they fail on the busiest day, and three cardless hours cost more than the reader. Check warranty terms and replacement lead times before committing to a brand.

The rule that matters

Buy the counter you can fix on a Saturday morning, not the one with the best spec sheet.

Frequently asked

Do I need an iPad, or will a cheap Android tablet do?

A cheap Android tablet runs the apps, but often loses security updates sooner and has weaker accessory support. If the tablet is the till, buy one that will still be supported in three years — or buy a sealed terminal.

Can a tablet POS take a deposit at booking?

Only if the reader and the booking software support stored cards or a payment link. Test that exact flow before buying hardware; it is the feature most often assumed and least often checked.

What fails first in a salon?

The charging port, then the cable, then the screen after a drop. A mount that charges without a cable solves the first two.
