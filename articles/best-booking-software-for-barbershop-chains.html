Best booking software for barbershop chains: multi-chair, multi-site

One shop runs on a calendar. Three shops run on permissions, transfers and a report an owner can actually read. That is where most barbershop software quietly falls apart.

What changes at chair three

A single-chair barber needs a booking page and a reminder. A chain needs four things that a cheap plan will not give you: per-chair calendars that can be viewed together, staff permissions that stop a new hire from editing prices, a walk-in queue that does not collide with online bookings, and one dashboard that totals revenue across shops. Miss any of those and the owner ends up doing spreadsheet work at 9pm, which is exactly what the software was supposed to remove.

The multi-chair view is what people underestimate. On a busy Saturday you want eleven columns at once, not eleven tabs. Test that first.

The four things that break in a second location

  • Client history. A regular who moves across town should keep their notes, their formulas and their card on file. If the platform treats each location as a separate account, they book as a stranger and you lose the relationship.
  • Staff transfers. Barbers move between shops more than any other trade. Moving a person should keep their commission history and future bookings, not force a re-entry.
  • Pricing per site. Rent differs, so prices differ. Platforms with only one price list force add-ons as a workaround, and your reports stop being comparable.
  • Reporting hierarchy. Shop managers need their own numbers; the owner needs the total. One login per shop plus one for the owner is the minimum.

The shortlist, by shop size

Two to four chairs, one or two sites: Booksy is the easiest rollout in this segment and has the client-side marketplace pull. Its multi-location tooling is thinner than the enterprise options, so check that transfers work the way you expect. Squire is built specifically for barbershops and handles the walk-in queue better than general salon tools.

Four to ten chairs across two to five sites: Vagaro and Mangomint are the practical picks. Mangomint's staff-facing interface is the fastest of the group in testing; Vagaro wins on breadth, including payroll and marketing add-ons you may already be paying someone else for.

Ten-plus chairs or a franchise plan: Phorest, Zenoti and Boulevard are the ones that survive an audit. They cost more per month and take longer to configure, and they are the only tier where per-location P&L is a built-in report rather than an export you rebuild in Sheets.

Rough pricing, checked November 2026

Expect $30 to $50 per chair per month in the small-chain tier, and $100 to $250 per location per month plus setup for the enterprise tier. Be wary of a flat monthly fee for eleven chairs — it usually means capped bookings or per-message charges later. Ask for the invoice total at your real volume, and what happens when you add a sixth site mid-contract.

What to test in the trial

  1. Book the same client at two locations from one profile. Check the history follows.
  2. Move a barber to another shop for a week. Check their future bookings and commission report.
  3. Take a walk-in while online bookings are open. Check nobody gets double-booked — our guide to handling double bookings covers the manual version of this problem.
  4. Export one month of revenue per site. If it takes more than ten minutes, the reporting is not ready.
  5. Turn off the owner's phone for a day. If the shop cannot run, the permissions are wrong.

Do not migrate mid-week

Move on a Monday, keep the old system read-only for two weeks, and re-book the next fourteen days by hand rather than trusting an import. Barbershop calendars are dense and a half-imported week costs more in goodwill than the subscription does in a year. The migration checklist has the sequencing we use.

Before you switch, also fix the two policies that generate most of the friction in a chain: who owns a no-show fee when a client books at the wrong shop, and how a late arrival is handled when the next chair is already occupied. Our no-show wording guide and the reminder comparison cover both.

The verdict

For two sites and under six chairs, Booksy or Squire, chosen on whether your walk-in traffic is heavy. For a growing local group, Mangomint if your barbers care about speed and Vagaro if you want one bill for everything. For a franchise plan or an investor-facing report, Zenoti or Phorest, and budget six weeks for setup. What you should not do is stay on a single-location plan with a shared login — that is the failure mode that costs a chain its Saturday.

Frequently asked

Can one booking page serve several barbershops?

Yes, but the location must be chosen before the service or the client will book the wrong shop. Put the location picker first, not last, and default it from the client's last visit.

Do chain plans include the walk-in queue?

Usually as an add-on above the base tier. Check it explicitly, because the queue is what most chains actually pay for and it is the first feature dropped in a cheaper plan.
