How to cut no-shows with automated reminders

Reminders are not a feature you switch on. They are a sequence, and most businesses run one message at the wrong time with the wrong wording.

Why one reminder does not work

A single "see you tomorrow" SMS is read in a queue with nine other notifications. It asks nothing, so the client does nothing. A no-show is rarely malicious — it is a forgotten intention. Your reminder has to create a decision point.

The sequence that works

48 hours before — the commitment message

Goal: force a yes/no while there is still time to refill the slot.

"Hi Anna, this is a reminder about your appointment on Thursday at 15:30 with Mark. Reply YES to confirm, or R to reschedule."

Track replies. Anyone who does not answer by the 24-hour mark gets a phone call if the slot is worth more than the call.

3 hours before — the logistics message

Goal: remove friction. Address, parking, doorbell, what to bring.

"Reminder: today at 15:30. We are at 12 Oak Street, parking behind the building. Reply if you are running late."

2 hours after a no-show — the recovery message

Do not shame. Offer the next slot.

"We missed you today. Want to grab Thursday 16:00 instead?"

This one recovers a surprising share of lost revenue and keeps the client.

Wording rules that matter more than timing

  • Ask for a reply. A message that requires an action gets read.
  • One question per message. Two asks halve the response rate.
  • Time first, place second. People scan for the number.
  • No marketing. Do not append an offer to a reminder; it turns the message into an ad.
  • Sender name, not brand name. "Mark from Studio Nine" beats "Studio Nine".

Deposits: the part nobody wants to do

Reminders reduce forgetfulness. Deposits reduce indifference. You need both.

A working policy: 20% or $10 (whichever is higher), charged at booking, refunded if cancelled 24 hours ahead, forfeited on no-show. Apply it to new clients and repeat offenders only — not to the client who has come every three weeks for two years.

What to measure

Track three numbers weekly: no-show rate, reminder reply rate, and rebooking rate after a no-show. If replies are under 30%, your wording is the problem. If no-shows stay above 10% with replies at 60%+, your deposit policy is the problem.

Tools that do this out of the box

Most booking platforms support a two-step reminder and deposits. If yours only sends one message, that alone is a reason to switch: Booksy, Fresha, Square Appointments all cover the basics.
