Best booking software for hair braiders: long appointments, deposits and prep

A braiding appointment is one client, one chair and most of a working day. Software built for thirty-minute cuts treats that block as an empty morning. Software built for braiders protects it.

Why braiding breaks a normal calendar

  • Duration is the product. Knotless medium at shoulder length is four hours; boho small at mid-back is eight. Same shop, same day, double the block.
  • The ticket is large and the day is fragile. At $180 to $400 a head, one no-show is a lost day, not a lost slot.
  • Hair is often part of the price. If you supply the bundles, the client pays for goods before the appointment starts.
  • Prep is part of the booking. Washed, detangled, blow-dried, no product. If that message arrives with the reminder instead of the confirmation, you lose an hour of the block.

The six features that decide it

  1. Per-service duration and price tiers. Length and size as separate options, each with its own block. A single "braids" service with a note field means every booking needs a phone call to price.
  2. A real deposit, collected at booking. Twenty to thirty percent, or a flat $40 to $60, taken by card inside the same tool that holds the calendar.
  3. Consultation with photo upload. Ten minutes, free or credited, with two reference photos. The biggest single reducer of four-hour arguments.
  4. Split or multi-session bookings. Wash day plus install day, or two sittings for a small boho set, both locked to the same client.
  5. Messages attached to the confirmation. Prep instructions, hair requirements, parking, whether to bring bundles.
  6. An enforceable cancellation window. Seventy-two hours for anything over four hours, so the deposit does the arguing for you.

Price the block, not the style name

The most common braider mistake is one price per style. Two clients ask for "knotless medium": one has waist-length density, the other fine shoulder-length hair. Same menu, same price, four hours apart in labour.

Build the menu as a grid: style, then length, then size, with the duration posted next to each cell and the calendar showing it. Clients self-select accurately when the price difference is visible, and the rest get caught at consultation.

The deposit question, honestly

Braiders resist deposits because the client base is loyal and the ask feels like distrust. The version that works is smaller than expected: a flat $50, applied to the balance, refundable up to seventy-two hours out. Low enough that regulars do not blink, high enough that a no-show hurts the client too.

If you supply hair, split it: a hair deposit at booking, refundable only until the order is placed, plus the service deposit. State both in the booking flow so nobody discovers the rule at the door. Our intake form guide covers what to ask while you still have their attention, and reminder timing matters more here than anywhere: 72 hours for prep, 24 hours for a yes-or-no.

Filling the gaps a long calendar leaves

Six-hour blocks create dead space: a two-hour hole between a takedown and an install, or a cancelled morning you cannot sell at full price. Two services fix this without discounting. Takedowns run sixty to ninety minutes, are priced separately, and are the natural first appointment for anyone booking an install. Consultations take ten minutes and are the only thing that fits a ninety-minute gap.

Set a waitlist on the long services so a cancellation pulls a real client forward instead of becoming a discount. If you teach, group bookings covers capacity and deposits for classes.

What I would pick

Solo, bookings mostly from Instagram: GlossGenius. Branded booking page, card on file, deposits, forms and photo-friendly client records, with rebooking prompts that suit a six-week retighten cycle. Paid, so treat it as a fixed cost from month one.

No budget yet: Fresha. Free core booking, deposits and card payments. You will chase confirmations by hand for a few months.

Already taking card payments: Square Appointments. One dashboard for money and calendar beats a second subscription, though the client records are thin for braiding.

Booth rent or two chairs: Vagaro. Per-staff calendars and commission reporting once the chair is shared. Booksy is worth a month's trial for marketplace discovery, but its booking page is the weakest for a braider whose clients already follow her.

Three mistakes that cost more than the subscription

  • One service, one duration. If the calendar cannot hold an eight-hour block, a double booking is a matter of time.
  • Booking in the DMs. Every Instagram booking is a thread you re-read, with no deposit, no reminder and no record.
  • No prep message. Hair that arrives unwashed at the door is an hour of the block gone, unpaid.

The test after two months: how many installs have a retighten date booked before the client leaves? Most of them, and the software is doing the work a receptionist would.

Frequently asked

How much deposit should a braider charge?

A flat $40 to $60 or twenty to thirty percent, applied to the balance and refundable up to 72 hours before a long appointment. If you supply hair, add a separate non-refundable hair deposit once the order is placed.

How do I stop no-shows on six-hour appointments?

Deposit at booking, prep instructions with the confirmation, and a confirmation request at 72 and 24 hours. A waitlist fills the slot when someone cancels anyway.

Should braiding prices be per style or per hour?

Per style, but split into length and size tiers with the duration shown for each. Hourly pricing is fairer in theory and much harder to sell; tiers get the same accuracy without the argument.
