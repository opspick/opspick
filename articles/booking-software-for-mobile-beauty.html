Booking software for mobile beauty therapists and makeup artists

Salon software assumes a fixed address and a waiting room. Mobile work has neither, and the gaps show up as late arrivals, double bookings and clients who never filled in the patch test.

What mobile work changes about a calendar

A salon appointment occupies a slot. A mobile appointment occupies a slot, a journey, and a parking space. Software that only understands the first one will quietly book you into an impossible day, and it will be your fault for trusting it.

  • Every appointment has an address. Not a notes field - a real field, captured at booking, shown on the day's list.
  • Travel time is part of the service. Between two addresses, not at the start of the day.
  • The client may not be the payer. A bride books for five people. The record has to survive that.
  • Preparation happens before you arrive. Patch tests, nail removal, hair washing, a clear table. Some of that has to be scheduled days earlier.
  • You are often offline. Basements, lifts, rural roads. The day's schedule has to work without a signal.

Tools that fit, and where each one strains

Most general booking platforms can be made to work. The question is how much manual correction you accept each morning.

  • Fresha. Free calendar, strong client app, and a service menu that handles add-ons well. Address capture exists but travel buffers need discipline, and the marketplace orientation suits therapists who want new local clients. Check current plans.
  • Booksy. Good reminders and deposits, familiar to clients in many markets, and a mobile app that works well one-handed. Its default setup is still built around a shop address, so treat location as a per-appointment field you fill every time. Check current pricing.
  • Square Appointments. The cheapest credible starting point, with reminders included and card payments handled in the same app you would use for a deposit. Group bookings are the weak spot. Check current plans.
  • A calendar plus a form. Google Calendar and a form tool will run a small mobile business honestly, as long as you block travel time yourself and keep deposits manual. It stops working at roughly the point where you cannot remember who paid.

Travel time and charging for it

Set travel buffers by zone, not by hope. A workable starting point for a city: 30 minutes between addresses in the same district, 45 across a river or a ring road, 60 for anything outside your usual area. Add 15 minutes at the start of the first appointment for setup and 15 at the end for packing down.

Then make the buffer visible to clients. If your booking page sells a 10:00 slot and you need to leave at 09:15, the client should see 10:00, not 09:45. Buffers belong inside the calendar, not in the appointment length.

Charging for the journey

Decide before you publish whether travel is included in a radius, charged per mile, or built into a zone price. Whichever you choose, say it in one sentence on the booking page and show it as a line item where possible. A surprise travel charge at the door is the most common complaint in mobile beauty, and it is entirely avoidable.

Bridal and group bookings

A bridal party is one job with several people and one decision-maker. Book it as a single block with a start time, a total duration, and a headcount, then list the names in the notes. Taking five separate appointments guarantees that one of them moves and the schedule collapses.

Take the deposit from one person, and take it early - bridal enquiries convert slowly and drift. A deposit also makes the trial a commitment rather than a coffee chat.
