SMS reminder services compared on cost and reliability

Almost every provider advertises a low per-message rate. Almost none of them advertise what happens when a message fails, or how much of your monthly bill is a floor rather than usage.

What "reliable" actually means

Reliability in SMS is not uptime on a status page. It is a set of behaviours you can test before you buy, and most of them are invisible in a pricing table.

  • Delivery receipts. A provider that reports "sent" but not "delivered" is telling you it handed the message to a carrier, not that a phone received it. Without receipts you cannot tell a bad number from a bad route.
  • Automatic retry. Carriers drop messages. A good provider retries once or twice before giving up, and tells you it did.
  • Opt-out handling. A proper implementation recognises STOP, STOPALL, and UNSUBSCRIBE, suppresses the number, and confirms the opt-out back to the client.
  • Sender identity. In the US and Canada this means a registered 10DLC brand and campaign, or a toll-free number with verification. Unregistered traffic gets filtered, and filtered messages look exactly like delivered ones on your dashboard.
  • Segment accounting. One long reminder becomes two or three billable messages. Providers that hide segmentation in the fine print are not lying, but they are not helping either.

The cost traps

Headline pricing is usually the least useful number on the page. These four decide what you actually pay.

  • Platform fee versus usage. Many "cheap per message" providers charge a monthly minimum that you pay whether you send 40 texts or 400. Divide the minimum by your real monthly volume before comparing.
  • Segments, not messages. A 200-character reminder with a name, a time, an address, and a reschedule link will often bill as two segments. Shorten the message before you blame the provider.
  • Inbound messages. Confirmations and reschedule requests are inbound. Some plans count them, some charge extra, some include them. Ask directly.
  • Number registration. US and Canadian 10DLC registration carries one-off and sometimes recurring carrier fees that are separate from your subscription.

How to test before you commit

  1. Send the exact reminder you plan to use, with a real client name, to five phones on three different carriers.
  2. Check the delivery receipt timestamp, not just the status. A receipt that arrives six hours later is a receipt you cannot act on.
  3. Reply STOP from one phone and confirm the number is suppressed, and that no further messages arrive.
  4. Send a message at your real sending time, which for most businesses is early evening. Route quality varies by hour.
  5. Run it for two weeks and count confirmed appointments, not delivered messages.

The boring recommendation

Use the reminders inside the booking tool you already pay for until you can show that wording or timing is costing you appointments. Then move to a dedicated provider, keep the message under 160 characters, register your sender properly, and track one number: confirmations per hundred reminders sent. Cost per delivered message is a procurement metric. Confirmations per hundred is the business metric, and it is the only one that survives a price increase.
