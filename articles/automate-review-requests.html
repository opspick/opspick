How to automate review requests without annoying clients

Most review requests fail for a boring reason: they ask at the wrong moment, through the wrong channel, with a link that takes four taps.

Timing

Ask 2-4 hours after the appointment, not the next day. The experience is still warm, and the client is still holding their phone. For services with a visible result (hair, nails, cleaning), 24 hours also works because the client has looked in the mirror.

One request only. A follow-up 48 hours later converts very little and costs goodwill.

Channel

SMS or the same channel the booking came from. Email review requests are the weakest option â€” open rates are low and the link rarely survives.

Wording that works

"Hi Anna â€” thanks for coming in today. If you have 30 seconds, a Google review helps us more than you'd think: [link]"

  • One sentence of thanks.
  • One honest reason ("it helps us").
  • One link, straight to the review box, not to your profile page.
  • No emoji spam, no discount attached.

The part everyone skips: the unhappy ones

If the client rated the visit below neutral at checkout, do not send the public review link. Ask one question internally, route it to the owner, and reply the same day. Public review flow for happy clients, private recovery flow for everyone else. That split is the difference between a 4.2 and a 4.8.

Where to automate it

Booking platforms with review automation: Booksy and Fresha can queue a request after a completed appointment; Square does it through its marketing add-ons. If your tool cannot, a simple automation from the booking webhook to an SMS provider covers it.

What to measure

Reviews per 100 completed appointments. Below 2 means timing or link friction. Between 3 and 6 is healthy for local services. Above that, you are probably asking twice.
