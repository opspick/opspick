Best POS for coffee shops: speed, modifiers, loyalty without apps

A coffee counter is not a restaurant. Every order is four choices at once and the transaction has to fit inside twenty seconds. Pick a till built for throughput, not for table maps.

What the till is actually for

In a cafe the point of sale does three jobs: it takes an order fast, it tells the barista exactly what to make, and it takes money without asking the customer to download anything. Everything else is a bonus you pay for only after those three are solid.

The six checks that matter before you buy

  • Two taps to a flat white. Size and milk should be buttons, not sub-menus. Count the taps on your four best sellers during a demo.
  • Modifier logic that cannot be skipped. If a drink must have a milk and a size, the till should not let it through without both.
  • Offline mode. Orders and card payments should survive a dropped line and settle later. Test it by unplugging the router.
  • Name or number on the ticket. The barista call has to match what is printed. Order-name fields are worth more than a loyalty module.
  • Tip handling. Card tips, pooled tips and the report your accountant needs. Check whether tips can be split by hours worked.
  • Fees that do not scale badly. A flat percentage is fine at low volume and expensive at high volume. Compare it against interchange-plus before you commit.

Most single-site cafes are best served by Square or a bank-supplied Clover, because a replacement reader is a same-day problem. Multi-site operators should budget for setup weeks, not just the subscription.

Modifiers are where tills quietly lose money

Every drink is a set of choices: size, milk, syrup, extra shot, temperature. If those live in a free-text note, the barista reads handwriting during a rush and the till cannot report that oat milk outsells almond. Make modifiers buttons with prices attached, so the common combination is one tap plus one tap, and check the receipt at arm's length: "LARGE OAT LATTE, 1 EXTRA SHOT", not a line of abbreviations.

Loyalty without forcing an app

The best loyalty programme in a cafe is a phone number at checkout plus a prepaid card. Ten drinks for the price of nine brings cash forward, and a phone number lets you text a quiet-week offer without an app nobody installs. Run that on top of the till instead of paying a monthly loyalty platform. The loyalty guide covers tiers and expiry; gift card pricing covers the accounting.

Hardware at the counter

Two screens beat one: a customer-facing display cuts "did that go through" questions and quietly lifts tips. Put the printer where the barista stands, not next to the till, and mount the tablet so it cannot walk off. The tablet POS guide covers stands, readers and what survives a wet counter.

The rule that matters

Buy the till that makes your four best-selling drinks two taps each, then spend the saved money on a spare reader.

Frequently asked

Do I need a POS that handles offline payments?

Yes, if you are in a building with unreliable broadband. Ask whether the reader stores transactions and settles later, then test it by disconnecting the router mid-service.

Is a flat-rate card fee cheaper than interchange-plus for a cafe?

It usually is below roughly 10,000 in monthly card volume, and usually is not above it. Check the effective rate you actually pay per month rather than the headline percentage. The processor comparison walks through the maths.

Can I run loyalty from the till without a separate app?

Yes. A phone number field at checkout plus a prepaid ten-drink card covers most of what a loyalty app does, and it costs nothing per month.
