Best booking software for veterinary clinics

A vet clinic sells fifteen-minute consultations and also absorbs whatever walks through the door bleeding. Any booking system that cannot survive the second half of that sentence will be abandoned by the second week.

Species, not services

A booking tool asks "which service?" A clinic asks "which animal, how heavy, how old, and is it a repeat visit for the same problem?" That is a different data model, and general tools will not hold it. They can still run the diary; they just cannot be the record.

  • Patient separate from owner — one household, three animals, three histories.
  • Appointment types by species and purpose — vaccination, post-op check, euthanasia consult.
  • Room and equipment — consult room, X-ray, ultrasound, isolation.
  • Vet and nurse together — some procedures need both, booked as one block.
  • Emergency capacity — a reserved slice of the day that online booking cannot take.

The three tools, honestly

Fresha

Free at the core and easy for pet owners, who are already used to booking groomers and walkers through apps like this. Client records are people-shaped: one record is one customer, so a household with two dogs needs a naming convention and discipline. No clinical notes, no dispensing, no species field worth relying on.

Booksy

Strong reminders, which a clinic genuinely benefits from — vaccination appointments are forgotten at a remarkable rate. Per-staff pricing is heavy for a clinic with four vets and three nurses, and the marketplace is not where pet owners look for a vet.

Square Appointments

The best of the three on payments, deposits and cancellation fees, and it handles multiple staff calendars cleanly. Resource booking exists. What it does not have is any notion of a patient that is not a person, or a prescription that needs repeating.

Walk-ins and the schedule that breaks

Every clinic day has a plan and every clinic day has an interruption. The scheduling question is not how to prevent emergencies, it is how much slack to leave so an emergency does not push the whole afternoon by forty minutes.

Two practical rules work better than any feature. First, keep online booking out of the first and last hour of the day, so there is room at both ends. Second, cap the number of bookable slots per hour rather than filling the grid — one held back per hour absorbs most of what arrives.

If the tool allows it, mark certain services as phone-only. A euthanasia appointment should never be bookable by a stranger at 2am through a widget, and a suspected blockage should be triaged by a person, not slotted into a fifteen-minute gap.

Reminders, repeats and multi-pet households

Vaccination reminders are the closest thing a clinic has to a subscription. Boosters come due on a schedule the owner does not track, and the reminder is often the only reason the appointment happens. A general booking tool can send a reminder if someone sets the date; it will not know that a dog is due in eleven months.

Repeat prescriptions have the same shape. The client needs a refill, the vet needs to authorise it, and the front desk needs to know whether a check-up is required first. None of the three tools do this. Keep it in your practice management system and use the booking layer only for the appointment.

Multi-pet households are where records quietly rot. Decide a convention on day one — owner surname plus animal name in the client field — and enforce it, because the alternative is two files for the same dog and a vaccination history split across both.

What I would do

If you already run a veterinary practice management system, do not replace it. Ask whether it has an online booking module; if it does, use that, because the record and the diary belong together. This is the one category where the general tools are a compromise rather than a shortcut.

If you are a small clinic or a mobile vet without a PIMS and need something this month: Square Appointments for the calendar, deposits and cancellation fees, with clinical notes kept elsewhere and backed up. Fresha is the cheaper start if you only need a diary and reminders.

Booksy is worth a look only if reminders are your main failure and your team is small enough that per-staff pricing stays low. In every case, check current pricing before you commit, and plan the migration so existing histories are not stranded.
