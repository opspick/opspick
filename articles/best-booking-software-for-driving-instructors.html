Best booking software for driving instructors

A driving instructor's day is a driving route with pupils in it. The diary has to know where each lesson starts, because the next one has to start somewhere a car can actually get to.

The diary is a route, not a grid

Most booking software shows a column of times. An instructor needs a sequence: pick up at 10:00 in one suburb, drop off at 11:30, next pupil at 12:00 across town. Ten minutes of travel is not a gap in the calendar, it is a constraint. Get it wrong and every lesson after it starts late.

  • Lessons of different lengths — one hour, ninety minutes, two-hour motorway sessions.
  • Pickup address on the booking — visible on the phone at 09:55.
  • Buffer between lessons — enforced, not just remembered.
  • Block bookings — ten lessons paid up front, with the balance tracked.
  • A cancellation window that suits driving — usually longer than a salon's.

The three tools, honestly

Fresha

Free, quick to set up, and fine for a solo instructor with a simple weekly pattern. Services can be one or two hours, deposits work, and pupils can rebook themselves. What it cannot do is plan a route or warn you that the 12:00 pickup is forty minutes from the 11:30 drop-off.

Booksy

Good reminders, which matters more here than in most trades — a pupil who forgets costs you the drive to their house as well as the lesson. Recurring weekly slots are workable. The marketplace brings enquiries from people looking for a haircut, not a driving test.

Square Appointments

Solid on payments, deposits and cancellation fees, which is the part of this job that generates the most friction. Calendar is clean. Again, no geography: it will happily let you book two pupils twenty miles apart with five minutes between them.

Block bookings and the money

Almost every instructor sells blocks: ten hours for a price, paid before the first lesson. The software needs to show how many hours remain, deduct them automatically, and refuse to book past zero. If you are counting in a notebook, you will eventually count wrong in your own favour, and that conversation is worse than any software fee.

Deposits are the other lever. A pupil who has paid something turns up. A pupil who has paid nothing and booked six weeks ahead is a coin flip. Take a deposit on the first lesson, and let the block take over from there.

Refund policy matters too. Blocks sold as "ten lessons" create an obligation; blocks sold as "ten hours, valid twelve months" create less. Write the version you can honour, then put it in the booking confirmation where the pupil actually sees it.

Test days and last-minute gaps

Driving test dates move. Pupils get a cancellation slot with two days' notice and want the car and the instructor for that exact morning. If your calendar cannot absorb a two-hour block at short notice, you either say no or you shuffle three other people. Both cost you.

The practical answer is a visible waiting list and a rule about who gets a moved lesson first. Some instructors keep Friday afternoons light for exactly this. Software will not decide that for you, but it will show you the pattern after a month.

Late cancellations are the same problem in reverse. A pupil cancelling at 07:00 for a 09:00 lesson has cost you the slot and the journey. A 48-hour window is common in this trade and easier to defend than 24, because the pupil's own test date gives them a reason to respect it.

What I would do

Solo instructor, steady weekly pupils, mostly blocks: Square Appointments for deposits and cancellation fees, with lesson blocks tracked as packages. The payment side is where it earns its place.

Just starting, a handful of pupils, no budget: Fresha, free tier, one-hour and two-hour services, and manual buffers in the calendar. Add Booksy only if reminders are the thing that keeps going wrong.

If you run two cars or teach full-time with a dense route, look at instructor-specific diary apps — they handle pickup geography and test-day swaps, which none of the three general tools do. Check current pricing on whichever you shortlist; the per-month fee is small next to one missed lesson a week.
