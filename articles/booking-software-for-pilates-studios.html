Best booking software for Pilates studios: reformer capacity, class packs and waitlists

A Pilates studio does not sell appointments. It sells one of eight places on a specific reformer at 7:15 on a Tuesday, paid for eleven weeks ago out of a pack of twenty.

Why appointment software collapses in a Pilates studio

Most schedulers are built for one client, one slot, one price. A studio runs the opposite model: a class is inventory with a hard cap, clients buy credit in advance, and the same person may hold a membership, a pack and a single drop-in at the same time. The four jobs that follow are the whole buying decision.

  • Capacity tied to equipment. Eight reformers means eight places. A ninth booking is not an oversell, it is a safety problem.
  • Class packs with an expiry. Ten classes for the price of nine, redeemable against a calendar that changes weekly.
  • A waitlist that fills gaps. The 6pm class is full; two people drop out on Friday. Nobody should be phoning anyone.
  • One policy for late cancels. Enforced by the software, not by a conversation at the front desk.

The five things that matter

  1. A real capacity field per class. Not a maximum party size buried in settings, but a number that the roster visibly respects.
  2. Credits deducted at booking, returned at cancellation. If staff do this by hand, it happens for two weeks and then stops.
  3. Automatic waitlist promotion. First on the list gets a text, has 30 minutes to claim, then it moves on.
  4. Late-cancel rules with teeth. Inside 12 hours, the credit is gone. Inside 4, a second offence costs a class.
  5. Instructor view on a phone. Who is new, who is pregnant, who is on a six-week postnatal block. On the reformer, not on a printout.

The short list

Momence - the strongest fit for a reformer studio

Momence was built for boutique studios: class schedules with capacity, credit packs, memberships, waitlists, instructor payouts and a booking app that clients actually use. Credit expiry and late-cancel rules are native rather than bolted on, and a waitlist promotion happens without staff. It handles multiple locations in one account, which matters the moment you open a second room. Pricing scales with bookings, so a full studio pays more than an empty one - fair, but budget for it.

bsport - the better fit once you teach several formats

bsport targets studios running Pilates, yoga and fitness side by side: memberships, packs, courses, workshops and a branded app. Its course and workshop handling is the best of this group, which is exactly what a teacher-training programme needs. Setup is heavier and the sales process is a demo call, so it rewards a studio with 200+ active clients and punishes a single-room operation.

TeamUp - the value pick for one room

TeamUp covers class capacity, memberships, packs and waitlists at a flat monthly price rather than a percentage, which makes it cheap at high volume. The client app is serviceable rather than lovely, and reporting is thinner than Momence. For a single-room studio with ten classes a day and a tight software budget, it does the job without a surprise invoice.

Mindbody and WellnessLiving - the incumbents

Mindbody wins on marketplace discovery: clients search for Pilates near them and find you. That reach is real, and it is also why the pricing is the highest here and the client experience is the most corporate. WellnessLiving is the usual alternative when you want the same feature set without the marketplace fees, with a stronger rewards system and a weaker app.

Square Appointments - only as a stopgap

Square Appointments is free for a solo instructor and fine while you teach one-to-one sessions on a single reformer. It has no credit ledger and no real waitlist, so the first group class ends the experiment.

Where each one breaks in month two

  • Momence: the per-booking price. A studio that fills every class pays for the privilege.
  • bsport: setup time. Expect a week of configuration before the first class goes live.
  • TeamUp: reporting and the app. "Which instructor retains clients" ends in an export.
  • Mindbody: cost and the client-side friction. Some clients hate booking through it.

Pick the weakness you can live with for a year. Switching mid-term loses packs and confuses regulars, and if you switch anyway, work through a migration checklist first.

Class packs: price them so they expire

A pack is a loan you owe in classes. Sell ten for the price of nine, give it twelve weeks, and make the expiry visible in the client's account from day one. Packs without expiry become a liability that grows every month and eventually forces an awkward conversation with your best client.

Two rules keep the ledger honest. Credits return only if the cancellation is outside your window, and the window is the same one the reminder message quotes. Anything else and the front desk becomes a tribunal. This is also why pack pricing should be modelled next to membership pricing: a pack sells commitment, a membership sells certainty, and most studios need both.

Waitlists are the cheapest revenue you have

A full 6pm class with four people waiting is not a success, it is a scheduling problem you can monetise. Turn on automatic promotion, give people 30 minutes to accept, and let the system text the next name. Studios that do this fill 60-80% of late drops without a single phone call - the same logic behind automated waitlists.

What to set up first

  1. One class type per format and duration: Reformer Flow 50, Reformer Basics 45, Prenatal 50. Not one generic "class".
  2. Capacity equal to the number of reformers in the room, set per class, not per location.
  3. The late-cancel window written in three sentences on the booking page and repeated in the confirmation.
  4. Waitlist promotion switched on before you run your first sold-out class, not after.
  5. An intake form attached to the first booking: injuries, pregnancy, surgery in the last six months.

For a single reformer room, Momence is the safe answer and TeamUp the budget one. If you teach Pilates, yoga and teacher training under one roof, start with bsport. The deciding question is not the feature list but this: when a class is full and two people cancel at 5pm, does your software fix it, or do you?

Frequently asked

How many places should a reformer class have?

As many as there are reformers, no more. Capacity belongs on the class, not the location, because a mat class and a reformer class in the same room hold different numbers.

Should class packs expire?

Yes. Ten classes in twelve weeks, visible in the client account. Packs without expiry turn into an open liability that grows every month.

Can I run a Pilates studio on Square Appointments?

Only for one-to-one sessions. Square has no credit ledger and no real waitlist, so group classes break it in the first week.
