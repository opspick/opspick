Booking software for mobile car detailing: what actually matters

A detailing booking is not an appointment. It is a van, a water source, a two-hour window at someone's house, and a car that may be twice as dirty as the photo suggested.

Why generic booking tools break at the driveway

Most booking tools were built for a chair in a room. They assume the client comes to you, that the service takes a predictable slot, and that nobody needs to know where the water comes from. A mobile detailer needs the opposite: the software has to qualify the job before it reaches your calendar, because the difference between a 90-minute maintenance wash and a four-hour paint correction is a whole afternoon of revenue.

The practical test is simple. Can the tool ask three questions before the slot is confirmed, refuse the booking if the answer is wrong, and take money at the same moment? If not, you are doing this by text message, and text message is where your margins go.

Travel radius is a pricing rule, not a map

Every mobile operator eventually learns this the expensive way: a booking 40 minutes away for a $120 wash loses money. The fix is to put distance into the price rather than into your temper. In the booking form, collect the postcode or ZIP and attach a travel band — free within 10 miles, a flat fee to 25, a quote request beyond that. Tools with service-area rules handle this automatically; Housecall Pro and Jobber both support service areas and per-area pricing, which is what you want if you run more than one van.

Then enforce travel windows. A morning slot in one town and an afternoon slot two towns over is a 40-minute dead drive you cannot bill. Block the calendar in half-days per area and let the booking page show only what fits. Most clients read "Thursday morning, your area" as more professional than a free-for-all calendar — see pricing mobile services for the fee language that keeps people happy.

The three questions that save the job

  • Water and power on site? Yes, no, or "there is a tap but no outdoor socket". This single answer decides whether you bring the tank and the generator, and it must be asked at booking, not on arrival.
  • Vehicle size and condition. Hatchback, saloon, SUV, van, or "very dirty". Condition tiers let you upsell honestly instead of arguing in a driveway.
  • Where will the car be parked? Driveway, street, underground garage. Garage work needs lighting and often a different machine; street work needs a permit conversation.

Three fields, under a minute. Anything longer and people abandon the form — the same rule that applies to client intake forms in every service business.

Deposits, because a driveway no-show costs a full tank

A no-show at a salon wastes 30 minutes. A no-show at a driveway two towns away wastes the drive, the slot, and the fuel. Take a deposit on anything above your base wash — 20% or a flat $25 is enough to make people reply to the reminder. Card-on-file works better than a payment link because it also covers the "car is not here" case. If you have not written the wording yet, no-show fee wording and deposit policy templates cover both the customer-friendly and the firm version.

Cash-only detailers: you can still take a deposit by card and settle the balance in cash on the day. That is covered in deposits with cash clients.

Photos are the receipt

Before-and-after photos attached to the appointment record end most disputes before they start. Look for a tool where the technician can upload from the phone directly to the job, and where the client receives them in the same thread as the invoice. If the software has no photo field, use a shared album link in the job note — crude, but it beats a WhatsApp thread you cannot search six weeks later.

Photos also do the marketing work. The best-performing post for a detailing business is a genuinely dirty interior next to a clean one, and you already have both on your phone.

What to pick, roughly

If you are one van and under 15 jobs a week, a field-service tool with a booking page is the honest answer: Jobber or Housecall Pro, both with scheduling, invoicing and card payments in one place, and both priced per user rather than per booking. If you are mostly maintenance washes with fixed prices, a lighter scheduler like Setmore or Square Appointments is cheaper and does the deposit plus reminder job. Build the comparison around travel rules and deposits, not around the number of features — a feature you never configure is a subscription you did not need. The one setting to change today

Turn on the deposit for every job above your base price, and add the water-and-power question to the booking form. Those two changes remove most of the driveway surprises, and neither requires switching software.

Frequently asked

Do mobile detailers need booking software or just a calendar?

If you take deposits and travel to clients, you need booking software. A calendar cannot collect payment, ask screening questions, or enforce a travel radius.

How much deposit should a mobile detailer take?

20% of the quoted job, or a flat $25 on smaller washes. It needs to be enough that the client replies to the reminder text.
