How to automate quotes: from enquiry to deposit

Most quotes are sent once and then chased for a week. The fix is not a better email. It is a flow where the client clicks, the deposit is taken, and the slot is blocked without you touching it.

A quote is a decision document, not an email

A quote has one job: get a yes or a no while the job is still fresh. Sent as a paragraph inside a mail thread, it asks the client to compare, decide, write back, and then wait for a payment link. Every step is somewhere to drop out, quietly.

Treat the quote as a page, not a message: options, a total, an expiry date, one button. If the client must compose a sentence to say yes, you have added a task to their day and a delay to yours.

Five things belong on a quote page, and nothing else:

  • What is included, line by line, priced per unit.
  • What is not included, in plain words. Exclusions are what clients remember, usually at the worst moment.
  • The total and the split. Deposit today, balance on the day, or staged dates.
  • An expiry date. Two weeks: long enough to be reasonable, short enough to matter.
  • One primary action. Accept and pay the deposit. Not three buttons, not a contact form.

The four steps that remove the chasing

The flow is short. What makes it work is that each step hands off to the next one automatically, so nothing waits on you remembering to send something.

StepWhat the client seesWhat happens without you
1. EnquiryA short form: what, when, where, budget rangeThe answer lands in one place, tagged by service type
2. QuoteA page with options, a total and an expiry dateSent within an hour of the enquiry, from a template you wrote once
3. ApprovalOne button that records the name and the timestampThe accepted quote becomes a job, an invoice or a booking
4. DepositA card field or a payment link, on the same pageThe slot is held, the calendar updated, the confirmation sent

Nothing there is clever: each step starts the next. Most small businesses own every piece and still lose days because a human forwards the emails between them.

Make approval a click, not an email reply

"Sounds good, let's go ahead" is not an approval you can invoice against. It does not say which version was accepted, and it is buried in a thread.

A button fixes both. It records who clicked, when, and against exactly which version. When the scope changes, you send version two and the old approval stays attached to the old numbers — that detail settles most scope arguments before they start.

Keep it short enough to read on a phone: total, expiry, deposit, what happens next. Long terms belong behind a link.

Bind the deposit to the approval

The gap between yes and money is where most quotes die. The excitement fades, and the deposit link arrives two days later in a separate email that looks like a bill.

  • Show the deposit on the quote itself, as a line with a number, not a surprise on the next page.
  • Send the client straight to payment after they accept. No second email.
  • Hold the slot for a stated window, then release it.
  • Chase twice, then stop. One reminder a day later, one at three days, then mark it lost.
  • Record the reason for a no. One dropdown on the decline button teaches you more than any dashboard.

If you take deposits by card, say on the quote whether it is refundable, how much is retained on a cancellation, and when it comes off the final bill.

Where the software helps, and where it stops

Square is the closest thing to a complete loop: an estimate goes out, the client accepts, an invoice follows, and the card payment clears in the same account. Simple one-line quotes are covered.

Fresha and Booksy solve the booking end and take deposits at the point of booking. They fit when the quote is a service menu and the client is choosing a slot, not negotiating a scope.

Neither is a full quoting system. For multi-stage projects with options and revisions, write the quote yourself and use the software only for approval and payment. That is the part costing you days.

Watch three numbers weekly: hours from enquiry to quote sent, share of quotes approved, and share of approved quotes paid within three days. If the third sags, the deposit step is the problem.
