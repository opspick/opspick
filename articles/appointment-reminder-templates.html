Appointment reminder templates: SMS and WhatsApp wording that gets replies

Most reminder messages fail for one boring reason: they inform without asking for anything. A reminder that ends in a question gets a reply, and a reply is what saves the slot.

Why your reminders get ignored

A message that reads "This is a reminder of your appointment tomorrow at 3pm" is a notification, not a conversation. The client reads it, thinks "I know", and puts the phone down. Nothing is confirmed, so the booking is still theoretical.

Three things fix that. Name the person and the exact time. State what happens if they cannot come. End with a question that has a one-word answer. Everything else - branding, emoji, long policy text - pushes the question off the screen.

The three-message sequence

Three touches is the practical ceiling. More than that and people tune out, or opt out entirely.

  • 48 hours out: confirm and offer the exit - this is where you recover slots from people who already know they cannot make it.
  • 24 hours out: reconfirm with logistics - address, parking, what to bring, how long it takes.
  • 2-3 hours out: short nudge only, for first-time clients or high-value appointments. Skip it for regulars who always show.

Copy-ready templates

48-hour template (SMS and WhatsApp)

Hi Sarah, it's Maya at Studio Nine. You're booked Thursday 14 Nov at 3:00pm for a 90-minute colour. Still good? Reply Y to confirm or N and I'll send you the next free slots.

Note the structure: sender, name, day and date, time, service, duration, question, two possible answers. Duration matters more than people think - it is the field clients most often get wrong.

24-hour template

Reminder: Sarah, tomorrow (Thu 14 Nov) 3:00pm at Studio Nine, 42 Bridge Road. Ring the top bell, there's parking behind the building. Running late? Just reply here. See you tomorrow.

This one carries logistics and nothing else. Do not repeat the price or the cancellation policy here; you sent it at booking, and repeating it reads as suspicion.

Same-day template

See you at 3 today, Sarah. Reply here if anything changes.

Under 60 characters, no link, no call to action beyond the reply. It exists to be read on a lock screen.

WhatsApp wording rules

WhatsApp is a chat app, so write like a person. No letterhead, no "Dear valued client", no paragraphs. One idea per message, three or four lines, and a clear question at the end.

  • Use the client's first name in the first line. It separates you from bulk senders instantly.
  • WhatsApp Business requires pre-approved templates for conversations you start. Keep the structure static, the wording casual.
  • Use quick-reply buttons where your tool supports them ("Confirm", "Reschedule"). Button taps beat typed replies.

SMS wording rules

SMS is 160 characters per segment and costs money per segment, which forces the message down to essentials.

  • Lead with the business name - that is all a locked phone shows.
  • Put the time and date before the address, and the question last.
  • One link maximum, and only to a reschedule page rather than your homepage.

Handling the reply

The reply is the whole point, so plan what happens next. A "Y" gets an automatic acknowledgment within seconds. An "N" gets a link to live availability, not a request to call during business hours. "Can I move it to Friday?" lands in a queue a human checks twice a day - that client is trying to keep the appointment.

If nobody owns the inbox, reminders are worse than useless: they promise responsiveness you do not deliver. Route replies into the same place your no-show reduction workflow lives, and check it before the first appointment of the day.

What to measure

Three numbers, tracked monthly:

  • Reply rate - share of reminders that get any response. Under 20% means the wording is wrong, not the timing.
  • No-show rate - compare clients who replied against those who did not. The gap is the value of the sequence.
  • Opt-out rate - above 1-2% per send means you are sending too often or too early.

Change one variable at a time, starting with the question at the end of the 48-hour message. That line usually moves reply rate more than any scheduling tweak.

Compliance in one paragraph

Collect consent at booking, keep a record of it, honour STOP immediately, and do not send between 9pm and 8am local time. WhatsApp Business adds template approval and opt-in requirements on top. Not legal advice, but those rules cover most of what gets small businesses into trouble.

Frequently asked

How many reminders should I send?

Two for regular clients (48 hours and 24 hours) and three for first-time or high-value appointments. Beyond three, opt-outs outweigh recovered slots.

Should the reminder ask for a reply?

Yes. A reminder that ends in a direct question - "Still good?" - gets far more responses than a statement, and replies are what let you resell a slot before it is lost.

Is WhatsApp better than SMS for reminders?

WhatsApp is cheaper and better for replies where clients use it; SMS reaches everyone without an app install. Most businesses use WhatsApp for the 48-hour confirm and SMS as the fallback.
