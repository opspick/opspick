Best payment processors for salons

The cheapest advertised rate is rarely the cheapest processor. What costs you money is the deposit that fails, the refund you cannot process, and the three days it takes to find out which appointment the payout belongs to.

Start with the question that actually matters

Are you choosing a processor, or choosing a booking system that happens to include one? Those are different decisions and they lead to different answers.

If your booking platform already takes cards, adding a second provider usually creates reconciliation work: two dashboards, two payout schedules, two ways for a refund to go wrong. If your platform is calendar-only, a standalone processor is fine and often cheaper at the margin. Decide this first, because it narrows the list more than any rate comparison.

Rates are deliberately absent from that table because they move constantly and depend on card type, volume, region and negotiation. Compare live quotes instead: ask each provider for an all-in effective rate on your actual monthly mix of card-present, online and keyed transactions.

Five costs that do not appear in the headline rate

  1. Deposit and no-show handling. Taking a card at booking is one thing; charging it later without the client present is another. Some processors treat that as a stored-credential charge with different rules, and some booking tools cannot do it at all. Test the exact flow before you commit.
  2. Refunds. A refunded deposit may not return the original processing fee. On a busy month of cancellations that difference is real money, and it is rarely quoted up front.
  3. Payout timing. Next-day, two-day and weekly payouts change your cash flow more than a few basis points of rate. Ask when the money lands, including weekends and holidays.
  4. Hardware and contracts. A terminal lease can cost more over three years than buying the reader outright. Never sign a multi-year terminal lease to get a lower rate without pricing the exit.
  5. Chargebacks and disputes. Salons attract disputes over no-show fees and "I did not authorise this" claims. Ask what the chargeback fee is, who writes the response, and whether the booking system supplies the evidence automatically.

What to check on the counter, not in the brochure

Take a real card and a real phone into the decision. Then check:

  • Does the reader connect reliably on the salon wifi, or does it need its own connection?
  • Can you add a tip at the counter and split it between staff without a second app?
  • Does a refund issued from the booking system reach the client's statement with a recognisable descriptor?
  • Can you see, in one place, the appointment, the payment and the payout that matches it?
  • What happens when the internet drops mid-transaction — does it queue offline or fail?

The descriptor matters more than people expect: an unrecognised charge is a chargeback waiting to happen.

A sensible shortlist by situation

Already using a booking platform with payments. Stay there unless the effective rate is clearly worse after you add the cost of a second system. Convenience has a price, and reconciliation time is part of it.

New salon, single chair, mostly card-present. A POS ecosystem with a cheap reader and a free booking tier is the lowest-risk start. Add online deposits once no-shows become a pattern.

Multiple staff and a real no-show problem. Prioritise stored cards and automatic no-show fees over the headline rate. One prevented no-show per week usually outweighs a small rate difference.

Retail plus services, or online sales. A gateway with a proper API and a booking tool that integrates with it beats a closed ecosystem, even if setup takes longer.

Whichever route you take, write the deposit and cancellation terms before you configure the processor, so the software enforces the policy you actually published. Deposit policy templates has wording you can adapt, and no-show fee wording covers the message clients see at booking.

If you want to compare the booking side first, Square Appointments, Booksy and Fresha all bundle payments with the calendar. Check current pricing and the deposit rules in your region before you choose.
