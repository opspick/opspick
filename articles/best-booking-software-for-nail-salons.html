Best booking software for nail salons

Nail salons live on three things booking tools usually handle badly: variable service length, add-ons mid-appointment, and walk-ins that arrive in a burst.

How the main options compare

Fresha

Strong on salons, free core, per-service duration editing is clean. Add-ons work as separate services, which is workable but clutters the menu. Best for solo or two-technician salons that want zero fixed cost.

Booksy

Best if your problem is discovery rather than scheduling. Marketplace traffic matters for new salons. Pricing is per staff, so a five-technician shop feels it.

Square Appointments

Good if you already take card payments with Square. Service variations and add-ons are more flexible than most, but the free tier is effectively one calendar.

The two settings that pay for themselves

Deposit only on long services

Charge a deposit on anything over 60 minutes. Short services stay frictionless, long slots stop disappearing. This single rule typically cuts lost hours by more than half.

Buffer time, not buffer anxiety

Set a 10-minute buffer after full sets and acrylics. It absorbs the overrun without pushing the whole day by an hour.

What I would do

New salon, one or two technicians: Fresha, deposits on 60+ minute services, 10-minute buffers.

Five technicians, empty weekday mornings: Booksy and treat the fee as marketing spend.
