Best booking software for tour operators: departures, capacity and weather calls

A tour does not sell an appointment. It sells sixteen seats on a 10:00 departure that may not run at all if the wind picks up. Standard salon calendars cannot express that.

Why an appointment calendar fails here

A booking tool built for services reserves one person for one client. A tour reserves a vehicle, a guide and a fixed number of seats on a fixed clock, then decides whether the departure happens based on weather, minimum numbers and guide availability. The unit is a departure, the limit is capacity, and the risk is a whole slot cancelling at once rather than one client calling in sick.

Book it in a staff calendar and the failures are predictable: seats oversold because two staff members each took a booking, pickup details scattered across WhatsApp, and a weather cancellation that has to be refunded one customer at a time.

The five features that decide it

  1. Departure grids with per-person capacity. 09:00 and 14:00 daily, sixteen seats each, sold per head. Filling 09:00 must not close 14:00, and the grid must show remaining seats rather than a binary free or busy.
  2. Minimum numbers. Plenty of tours only run with four or six passengers. The software should hold the departure as provisional and tell you which ones are short, instead of you checking a spreadsheet the night before.
  3. Pickup and meeting points. Free text for the meeting point, plus a dropdown of hotel pickups if you run them. This single field removes most of the "where exactly?" messages.
  4. Weather cancellation as a bulk action. One click to cancel a departure, refund or rebook every booking on it, and send one message to all of them. If that takes an hour, you will delay the call and lose the day.
  5. Reseller inventory. If you sell through hotels, tourist desks or online travel agencies, their bookings must land in the same inventory pool. Double-selling through a reseller is the mistake that gets you delisted.

The platforms worth looking at

FareHarbor is the default for small and mid-size operators. Departures, per-person pricing, pickup lists and a reseller network come with it, and the booking flow is designed for tourists on a phone. Pricing is commission-based on bookings rather than a monthly fee, which suits seasonal businesses with quiet months.

Rezdy is the other name you will hear constantly, and it is stronger on distribution: it connects to a large agent network and gives you a marketplace view of who is selling what. If your growth plan involves resellers rather than your own site, this is the one to price first.

Checkfront treats each departure as inventory with add-ons, which works well when you sell extras (lunch, a photo package, equipment hire) alongside the seat. Its flat monthly pricing is easier to forecast than commission.

Xola and Peek are aimed at activity operators with a strong front-desk and phone-booking workflow; both handle waivers and per-head pricing cleanly. Bokun is worth a look if you sell mostly through OTAs and want the channel manager more than the storefront.

None is free, but each costs less than one oversold departure.

Weather: write the rule before you need it

The policy that saves your reviews is boring and specific: if the operator cancels, the customer chooses a full refund or a free rebooking, and the decision is made by a stated hour (17:00 the day before is common for morning tours). Customer cancellations follow different rules, visible before checkout rather than in a PDF.

Put the weather clause on the booking page, in the confirmation email and in the reminder the evening before. Our guide to cancellation windows covers the revenue math; the tour version simply adds a second cancellation cause that is nobody's fault.

Deposits, groups and the awkward middle

Private tours and corporate groups want a hold weeks out, which means a deposit and a headcount deadline. Take the deposit at booking, set the final number 48 hours before departure, and state what happens to no-shows on the day: a seat paid for is a seat not argued about.

Group bookings deserve their own page in the software rather than a phone call and a note. The capacity and deposit logic is the same as any group booking, just with a harder cutoff because the vehicle leaves on time.

Pickups are a scheduling problem, not a messaging one

Every hotel pickup adds minutes to the route. Give each one a time window in the confirmation, so the customer waits at the door at 08:35 instead of messaging at 08:40. A late pickup is the tour version of a late arrival: publish the rule or every other passenger pays for it.

Price the season, not the day

Tours have three seasons in most cities: peak weeks where you can charge full rate and still sell out, shoulder weeks where demand is soft, and closed weeks. Put those rates in the software as date ranges instead of editing prices by hand each month. Our guide to seasonal pricing has the publishing pattern; the tour-specific add is early-bird pricing for departures more than two weeks out, which fills the quiet grid.

A realistic first month

Week one: departures, capacity and the minimum-numbers rule. Week two: pickup points in confirmations. Week three: the weather clause and the bulk cancellation action, tested on a fake departure. Week four: reseller connections, one at a time, with a test booking each.

Frequently asked

Can I use Calendly or Google Calendar for tours?

Only for private, one-group bookings. Neither can hold sixteen seats per departure, show remaining capacity, or cancel a whole departure in one action, so they break as soon as you run a scheduled tour.

Who pays the processing fee on a weather refund?

Usually you do, because most processors keep the original transaction fee on a refund. Price the tour so a rare full cancellation is survivable, and prefer a free rebooking option over a refund when the customer is still in town.

Do I need a waiver for a walking tour?

For low-risk walking tours, a short acknowledgement is usually enough. For anything with vehicles, water, heights or equipment, collect a signed waiver per participant at booking, one link per group. The field logic is the same as any intake form: fewer fields, higher completion.

How far ahead should I open departures?

Ninety days is a practical default for tours people book around flights, with early-bird pricing for the first thirty days of that window. Beyond a season, you are guessing at costs.
