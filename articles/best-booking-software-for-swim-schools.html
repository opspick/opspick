Best booking software for swim schools: lanes, make-up lessons and term billing

A swim school does not sell appointments. It sells a place in a lane, once a week, for twelve weeks, to a child who will change level halfway through and whose mother books for two siblings on one card. Very few booking tools can describe that sentence.

Why a salon calendar collapses in week two

A normal calendar reserves one person for one client. A swim class reserves space in a lane for six to eight children, with an instructor ratio from your insurance policy, at a fixed weekly time, for a term. The unit of capacity is a lane slot; the constraint is bodies in the water.

Book it in a staff calendar and the failures arrive in order: the 4pm class shows six places when the ratio is four, a child moves up a level but keeps the old slot, and make-ups live in a notebook nobody can find in March.

The six features that decide it

  1. Class capacity per slot, not per instructor. "Max 5 swimmers" on a class must refuse the sixth whatever the roster says.
  2. Levels attached to the child. A swimmer moves from Starfish to Octopus mid-term; class level and child level must agree before booking.
  3. Recurring enrolment for a term. Twelve weeks, same day, one invoice — not twelve bookings or a subscription that rebills in January.
  4. Family accounts. One payer, several children, one invoice and one login.
  5. Make-up credits with rules. Earned on a miss, usable on another day, expiring after set weeks, never refundable.
  6. Waitlists that convert. Ordered, time-stamped, with offers that expire.

The tools worth looking at

Jackrabbit Class is the default answer for swim schools in North America: capacity, levels, skills tracking, family billing and make-up credits are native. The interface is dated and setup is a project, but the enrolment model matches how a swim school sells.

iClassPro covers the same ground with a cleaner parent portal and better retention reporting, priced per student — fine at 300 swimmers, painful at 40.

Amilia is activity registration software, the model municipal programmes use. Good if you also run camps and holiday intensives through one checkout.

Mindbody and Zen Planner handle class capacity and memberships well, and are common in pools that also run adult aqua fitness. Neither tracks stroke progression; keep that in a sheet.

If you only run squad training and race meets, SwimTopia is a different category — meet entries, not lane places.

Make-up lessons: the policy decides the software

A child misses Tuesday because of a school trip; the parent wants Thursday instead. Say yes without a rule and Thursday is oversubscribed by week six, breaking the lane ratio.

Decide three numbers before you shop: make-ups per term, how long a credit stays valid, and whether it can be used in another level. Then check the software enforces all three without a spreadsheet. Our late cancellation guide covers the same trade-off for one-to-one services.

Term billing without a January revolt

Swim schools bill in blocks: ten to fourteen weeks, up front or in two instalments. Parents accept that; they do not accept a surprise rebill in the holidays or a charge while the pool is closed.

Set term dates in the software, invoice per family rather than per child, and mark pause weeks as closed days. A tool that cannot exclude weeks from a recurring enrolment will charge for lessons you are not teaching. Our guide to membership pricing models covers when a rolling plan beats a term.

Trials, ratios and the front desk

Most new swimmers start with a trial or assessment. Book it as a short slot with a note field for the instructor's level call, then convert it to a term place in one action — otherwise the front desk re-enters the child by hand.

Reminders matter more here because the customer is not the person attending. Send them to the paying parent and keep the message short: pool, time, what to bring. No-show recovery differs too — a missed class is a credit, not a fee.

Test one detail before you buy: can an instructor mark a register on a tablet at poolside in under a minute? Attendance entered later from memory makes make-up credits wrong.

What to ignore while choosing

  • Point-of-sale hardware bundles. You sell a term, not a retail basket.
  • Marketing suites. Parents leave because the time changed, not for lack of email campaigns.

A realistic first month

Week one: lanes, class slots, ratios. Week two: levels and the assessment-to-enrolment path. Week three: family billing and term dates. Week four: make-up rules and the waitlist. Switch at a term break — the migration checklist has the order.

The month-end test: can a parent book two children into two levels, pay once, and see last week's make-up credit without calling you? If not, you bought a calendar with a swimsuit on.

Frequently asked

Can I run a swim school on Calendly or Google Calendar?

Only for private one-to-one lessons. Neither can express a class with five places, a level requirement, family billing and make-up credits, so group lessons break them in the first week.

How many make-up lessons should a term include?

One or two, valid four to six weeks, bookable only into classes with a free place. Publish the rule before the term starts.

Should I bill per term or monthly?

Per term if the pool closes for holidays or maintenance, since you can exclude those weeks honestly. Monthly suits year-round programmes with a fixed slot.
