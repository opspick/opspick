Best booking software for music teachers: term billing, make-up lessons and family accounts

A music teacher sells the same 4pm Tuesday slot for fourteen weeks, to a family with two children, paid by bank transfer in two instalments. Almost no booking tool is built around that sentence.

Why a lesson studio is not a salon

Most schedulers assume a client books once, pays, and leaves. Lessons run the other way: the slot is the product, it recurs weekly, and money arrives separately from the booking. Four jobs follow, and a generic calendar does none of them.

  • Recurring slots that survive a term, with holidays paused rather than cancelled one by one.
  • Family accounts, because one parent pays for two children on different instruments at different times.
  • Make-up credits, since a missed lesson is neither a refund nor a free lesson.
  • Term invoices raised in advance with a due date and one reminder.

The five things that matter

  1. A repeating slot the parent can see. "Tuesdays 4:00pm, term ends 12 Dec" on a link, not in an email they will lose.
  2. Credits, not refunds. Notice given becomes a credit with an expiry date. Without a visible balance, every absence is an argument.
  3. One invoice per family. Three children should mean one transfer, not three sets of fees.
  4. Attendance that feeds billing. Marking a lesson attended should already update the invoice.
  5. Lesson notes the student keeps. What to practise this week, one paragraph. This is what parents repeat when they recommend you.

The short list

My Music Staff - the default for a solo studio

My Music Staff is built only for music teachers: recurring weekly slots, family accounts, attendance, make-up credits, invoicing and a student portal are all first-class features, with an instrument field on the student record. Pricing is per teacher and modest, and the trial is long enough to run a real term. The interface looks like 2014 and the parent mobile view is basic, but nothing else here is as complete for one-to-one lessons.

TeachWorks - the better fit once you have staff

TeachWorks was built for tutoring companies and music schools with several teachers: availability, substitutes, payroll hours, family billing and a parent portal. If two teachers share a room and a Google Calendar, this is the upgrade that ends double-booking. It costs more and takes an afternoon to set up, so it is wasted on a single teacher with fifteen students.

Duet Partner - the cheapest real parent portal

Duet Partner covers scheduling, attendance, invoices and practice notes at a lower monthly price. Deep reporting and payroll are missing. For a teacher whose main complaint is parents texting "what time is the lesson again", it is the value pick.

Square Appointments or Calendly - only as a stopgap

Square Appointments is free for a solo operator and takes card payments in the same flow; Calendly publishes availability in ten minutes. Both handle a repeating event and neither understands credits, family invoices or attendance. Fine for month one, a dead end by term two.

Where each one breaks in month two

  • My Music Staff: group classes. Capacity and waitlists are weak, so a five-student theory class needs manual care.
  • TeachWorks: price and setup. You pay for payroll you do not use.
  • Duet Partner: reporting. "Which instrument earns?" ends in a spreadsheet export.
  • Square: the credit ledger. Every make-up becomes a note you will eventually forget.

Pick the weakness you can live with for a year. Switching mid-term is worse than any missing feature, and if you switch anyway, use a migration checklist.

Make-up lessons: the policy does the work

The software only automates a rule you already decided. Write it once and put it on the booking page: 24 hours' notice earns a credit, less does not, credits expire at the end of the following term, and make-ups go into existing free slots. Then let the tool hold the balance.

Two details prevent most arguments. Credits must expire, or they become a debt you can never schedule. And a no-show is not a cancellation, so the wording should say so - the same distinction that makes a 24-hour reminder sequence work anywhere else.

Term billing without spreadsheets

Bill per term: a ten-week block at a fixed fee, invoiced in advance, split into two or three instalments if the family asks. Generate the invoice on day one, due in seven days, one reminder at day five, and pro-rate only the first invoice for a mid-term join. This is also why membership pricing beats hourly billing here - a flat fee for a guaranteed slot kills the "we only came twice this month" conversation.

What to set up first

  1. One service per instrument and duration: piano 30, piano 45, violin 30. Not one generic "lesson".
  2. Term dates entered as a season, so holidays pause the calendar in one action.
  3. A family account for every household with more than one student, before the first invoice.
  4. The make-up policy in three sentences on the booking page and in the confirmation.

For individual students, My Music Staff is the safe answer. If you are hiring a second teacher this year, start with TeachWorks - it costs more now and much less later. The deciding question is not the feature list, but which tool lets a parent see the slot, the balance and the invoice without calling you.

Frequently asked

Per lesson or per term?

Per term, invoiced in advance, split into instalments if needed. The guaranteed weekly slot is the product; hourly billing invites the "we missed two weeks" discount request.

What is a fair make-up policy?

24 hours' notice earns a credit, less does not, credits expire at the end of the following term, and make-ups fill existing gaps.

Can I just use Calendly?

Temporarily, for a handful of students. It cannot track credits, family invoices or attendance, so the admin returns the first week someone is ill.
