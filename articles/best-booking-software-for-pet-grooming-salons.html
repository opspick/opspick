Best booking software for pet grooming salons

In a grooming salon the client has four legs, cannot read a reminder, and is usually brought in by someone who is already late.

The pet is the client

That single fact changes what the software has to do. Every booking is really a record for an animal: breed, coat, temperament, skin, the last groom, what happened last time. If the tool only stores the owner's name, the groomer rebuilds that knowledge from memory every eight weeks — and every new groomer starts from zero.

  1. A pet record, not just a client record — one owner, two dogs, separate histories.
  2. Size-based services — small, medium, large, giant, with the price attached to the size.
  3. Notes that carry forward — temperament, matting, skin issues, "no dryer on the face".
  4. Deposits for new clients — a no-show costs a slot the groomer cannot refill.
  5. Pickup time in the confirmation — so the owner knows when to come back.

Notes that are worth writing down

Good groomers keep a mental file on every dog. The software should hold it instead: coat condition, how the dog behaved, what worked, what to avoid, any skin or ear issue worth mentioning to the owner. Add the vet's name and number once, so nobody scrolls through WhatsApp at the worst possible moment.

Two rules keep notes useful. Write them immediately after the groom, in the same fields every time, and keep them factual — "nipped when touched near left ear" is useful, "difficult dog" is not. If a dog has a history of biting, that note should be visible at booking, not discovered at pickup.

Price by size, not by guess

The most common pricing mistake in grooming is quoting on the phone. "A cockapoo, medium" can mean anything from a tidy-up to a full strip, and the client remembers the lower number. Put the size bracket on the booking page, with the price attached, and let the extras be clearly optional.

BracketWhat it changesAsk at booking
SmallShorter handling time, smaller tableBreed, weight, last groom date
MediumLonger drying, more coatMatting, behaviour on the table
LargeTwo-person handling sometimesLift help, vehicle access, anxiety
GiantLongest slot, specialist equipmentWhether you take them at all

Put your own prices next to each bracket and keep them visible. When a client asks "how much for a golden retriever", the answer is a link, not a conversation — and the conversation, when it happens, starts from an agreed number.

No-shows, late pickups and the awkward conversation

A grooming slot is hard to fill at short notice, and a reminders.html">no-show is a whole afternoon of income gone. The fix is boring and it works: a deposit for first-time clients, refundable with a day's notice, stated on the booking page in one sentence. Regulars do not need it.

Late pickup is the other leak. A dog left for an extra hour is a dog taking up a kennel, a staff member staying late, and the next appointment starting behind. Write a pickup window into the confirmation message and charge for extended boarding if your terms allow it. Say it up front, once, and then actually apply it.

Reminders do the heavy lifting here. One message a few days before with a confirm link, one on the morning with the drop-off time and a note about parking. Owners forget; the software does not have to.

The three tools, honestly

Booksy

Per-staff calendars, client records with notes, deposits and forms. It handles a multi-groomer salon well and the marketplace can bring in new clients. The per-staff monthly fee and marketplace commission are the costs to model — a salon with four groomers pays for four.

Fresha

Free core, good client records, deposits through their payment processing, marketplace included. A sensible start for a one- or two-groomer salon. If you need a separate record per pet rather than per owner, check how it handles multiple animals under one client before you move your book over.

Square Appointments

The easiest choice if the salon already takes card payments on Square. Deposits, no-show fees and staff calendars are there on the paid tier. No marketplace, and pet-specific fields are whatever you make of the notes box.

What I would do

One or two groomers, mostly regulars: Fresha with size-based services, a deposit for new clients, two reminders.

Busier salon with several groomers: Booksy for staff calendars, client notes and the marketplace, or Square Appointments if payments already run through Square.

Wherever you land, spend the first week on pet records and size pricing. The calendar is the easy part; remembering that the brown terrier hates the dryer is the part that keeps clients.
